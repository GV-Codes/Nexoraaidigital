Most people only learn the value of a backup after losing something. Here's a rule simple enough to follow before that happens.

## The 3-2-1 rule

- **3** copies of your data: the original plus two backups
- **2** different types of storage, such as your computer and an external drive
- **1** copy stored offsite, like a cloud backup or a drive at another location

## Why each part matters

Two copies in the same place can both be lost to a fire or a theft. Two on the same type of device can fail for the same reason. An offsite copy covers disasters.

## Sync is not a backup

Services that sync folders across devices mirror your changes, including mistakes. Delete a file or let ransomware encrypt it, and the damage may sync everywhere. Version history helps, but a true backup is a separate copy that doesn't change when your files do.

## Protect against ransomware

Keep at least one backup that isn't permanently connected to your computer, like an external drive you unplug or a cloud backup with versioning. Ransomware can't encrypt what it can't reach.

## Test your backups

A backup you've never restored is a hope, not a plan. Try recovering a file every few months.

## Try it today

Set up automatic backups for your documents and photos, then restore one file to confirm it works.
