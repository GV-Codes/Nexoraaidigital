Ask five people what a business analyst does and you'll get five answers. Here's a simple one: a business analyst helps an organization work out what needs to change and makes sure the change actually solves the problem.

## The bridge role

BAs sit between the people who have a need (sales, finance, customers, operations) and the people who deliver the solution (developers, vendors, project teams). They translate in both directions.

## What the work looks like

- Talking to stakeholders to understand problems and goals
- Mapping how processes work today and where they break
- Writing requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria
- Analyzing data to support decisions
- Facilitating workshops and keeping people aligned
- Checking that what's built matches what was needed

## Skills that matter

- **Listening and asking good questions** matters more than any tool
- **Clear writing,** since requirements are only useful if people understand them
- **Structured thinking** to break messy problems into parts
- **Basic data skills,** such as spreadsheets and SQL
- **Diplomacy,** because stakeholders rarely agree at first

## Where BAs work

Nearly everywhere: banks, hospitals, software companies, government, retail. Titles vary, including systems analyst, product analyst and requirements engineer.

## Try it today

Think of a process at your own job that frustrates people. Write down who is affected, what the problem is, and what a better outcome would look like. That's a BA's starting point.
