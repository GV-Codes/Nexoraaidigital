You can do flawless analysis and still lose the room. Charts alone don't persuade people. A clear message does.

## A simple structure

1. **Context:** why are we looking at this?
2. **Insight:** what did we find?
3. **Action:** what should we do about it?

## Lead with the answer

Don't walk your audience through your process and hope they reach the conclusion. Start with it: "Customer complaints doubled after the March update. Here's why, and here's the fix I'd suggest."

## One chart, one message

Each slide or section should make a single point. Use the title to state it. Compare:

- Weak: "Returns by month"
- Strong: "Returns doubled after the packaging change"

## Guide the eye

Highlight the one bar or line that matters and mute the rest. Add a short annotation on the chart itself.

## Know your audience

An executive wants the decision and the risk. A fellow analyst wants the method. Prepare both, and lead with the one your audience needs.

## Cut ruthlessly

If a chart doesn't support your message, move it to an appendix. Everything you leave in competes for attention.

## Try it today

Take your latest report and rewrite its chart titles as full sentences stating the finding.
