Every project has people who can help it, people who can block it, and people who simply need to know what's happening. Treating them all the same wastes effort and annoys everyone.

## Step 1: List everyone affected

Think beyond the obvious: users, managers, IT, legal, finance, customer support, even other teams that rely on the same system.

## Step 2: Plot them on a grid

For each person or group, ask two questions: how much **power** do they have over the outcome, and how much **interest** do they have in it?

- **High power, high interest: manage closely.** Involve them in decisions.
- **High power, low interest: keep satisfied.** Brief updates, no surprises.
- **Low power, high interest: keep informed.** They're often your best source of feedback.
- **Low power, low interest: monitor.** Light touch.

## Step 3: Plan how to engage

For each key stakeholder, note what they care about, what they need from you, how often to talk, and how they prefer to communicate.

## Revisit it

People move on the grid as a project evolves. A quiet executive can become very interested the week budgets are reviewed.

## A word of warning

Don't ignore the low-power groups who'll use the thing every day. They'll tell you whether it works.

## Try it today

List ten stakeholders for a current project and place each on the grid. Check whether your communication matches where they landed.
