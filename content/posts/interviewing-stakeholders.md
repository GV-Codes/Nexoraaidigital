A stakeholder interview isn't a form to fill out. It's a conversation where you're trying to understand a person's work, problems and priorities. Done well, it uncovers things nobody thought to write down.

## Before the interview

- Learn about their role and what they own.
- Tell them the purpose and how long it will take.
- Prepare a handful of questions, but don't read them like a script.

## Open with context

Ask them to describe their work in their own words: "Walk me through a typical week," or "Show me how you do this today." Stories reveal what bullet points hide.

## Questions that dig deeper

- "What's the hardest part of this?"
- "What happens when it goes wrong?"
- "Can you give me a recent example?"
- "Why do you do it that way?"
- "What would a perfect result look like?"
- "Who else should I talk to?"

## Listen more than you talk

Let silences sit. People often add the important detail after a pause. Avoid leading questions like "Wouldn't a dashboard solve that?" because they teach people to agree with you.

## Close the loop

Summarize what you heard and check you got it right. Send a short written recap within a day, and note any follow-ups.

## Try it today

Schedule a 20-minute conversation with someone who uses a process you want to improve. Ask only "walk me through it" and "what's the hardest part?"
