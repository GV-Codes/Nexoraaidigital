The best chart isn't the fanciest. It's the one that makes the answer obvious in a few seconds.

## Match the chart to the question

- **Comparing categories?** Use a bar chart.
- **Showing change over time?** Use a line chart.
- **Showing parts of a whole?** Use a stacked bar, or a pie chart only if you have a few slices.
- **Looking for a relationship between two numbers?** Use a scatter plot.
- **Showing how values are spread out?** Use a histogram.

## Habits to drop

- **3D effects.** They distort what you see.
- **Truncated bar charts.** Bars should start at zero, otherwise small differences look huge.
- **Two different y-axes.** They can suggest relationships that aren't there.
- **Too many colors.** If everything stands out, nothing does.
- **Pie charts with ten slices.**

## Make it readable

- Sort bars from largest to smallest unless there's a natural order.
- Label directly on the chart rather than forcing people to check a legend.
- Write a title that states the message: "Returns dropped 20% after the new packaging" beats "Returns by month."
- Cut gridlines and decoration that don't help.

## Try it today

Pick a chart you made recently and ask what question it answers. If you can't say it in one sentence, simplify it or change the chart type.
