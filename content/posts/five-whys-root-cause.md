When something goes wrong, the first explanation is rarely the real one. The Five Whys is a simple habit: ask "why?" again and again until you reach something you can actually fix.

## An example

*Problem:* Customers received the wrong item.

1. **Why?** The wrong product was packed.
2. **Why?** The picker grabbed the item next to the right one.
3. **Why?** The two products have nearly identical labels.
4. **Why?** The supplier changed its packaging and we never updated our shelf tags.
5. **Why?** There's no process for checking what changes when suppliers update products.

Telling the picker to be more careful wouldn't have solved anything. Creating a supplier-change check does.

## Tips

- **Focus on process, not blame.** If a "why" ends at a person's name, keep going.
- **Use facts.** Check each answer rather than guessing.
- **Don't treat five as magic.** Stop when you reach a cause you can act on, which may take three or seven.
- **Involve the people close to the work.** They know the details.
- **Watch for several causes.** Sometimes the chain branches.

## Then act

Pick a fix for the root cause, assign an owner, and decide how you'll know it worked.

## Try it today

Take a recurring annoyance at work and ask why five times. Write down where the chain ends.
