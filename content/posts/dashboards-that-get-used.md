Plenty of dashboards launch with a flourish and then quietly die. Nobody opens them. The problem is rarely the charts. It's the planning.

## Start with decisions, not data

Ask the people who will use it: what do you decide each week, and what number would help? Build around those answers rather than around what's easy to chart.

## Keep it focused

- Aim for five to seven visuals, not twenty.
- Put the most important number at the top left, where eyes land first.
- Show each number against something: a target, last period, or a benchmark. A figure with no comparison is hard to interpret.

## Make definitions consistent

If "active user" means one thing here and another in a different report, trust evaporates. Write definitions down and put them where people can see them.

## Plan the upkeep

- Decide how often the data refreshes and say so on the dashboard.
- Name an owner who answers questions and fixes problems.
- Check usage after a month. If nobody looks at a dashboard, retire it.

## Test with real users

Watch someone try to answer a real question with your dashboard. Where they hesitate, redesign.

## Try it today

Look at an existing dashboard and ask its users which two numbers they actually look at. Consider removing the rest.
