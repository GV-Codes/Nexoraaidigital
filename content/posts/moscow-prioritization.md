When everything is a top priority, nothing is. MoSCoW gives a team a shared language for deciding what really has to ship.

## The four categories

- **Must have:** without it, the release fails or isn't usable. Non-negotiable.
- **Should have:** important, but there's a workaround if it slips.
- **Could have:** nice extras if time and budget allow.
- **Won't have (this time):** agreed to be out of scope for now. This one prevents arguments later.

## A quick test for "Must"

Ask: "If we launched without this, would it still be worth launching?" If the answer is yes, it's not a Must.

## Keep Musts under control

A common guideline is to keep Musts to about 60% of the effort, so you leave room for surprises. If everything lands in Must, push back and ask what's truly critical.

## Run it with the group

1. List the requirements.
2. Let stakeholders propose categories, then discuss disagreements.
3. Record the reason for each Must, since it helps when someone wants to change it.
4. Revisit as the project moves.

## It's about this release

"Won't have" doesn't mean never. It means not now, and that clarity protects the schedule.

## Try it today

Take your current to-do list or backlog and sort the top 15 items into the four buckets. Count how many ended up as Musts.
