"The average" sounds like one number, but there are three common ones, and they can tell very different stories.

## The three

- **Mean:** add everything up and divide by how many there are.
- **Median:** the middle value when you line them up in order.
- **Mode:** the value that appears most often.

## An example

Seven salaries, in thousands: 40, 45, 48, 50, 52, 55 and 400.

- The **mean** is about 98.6. The one huge salary drags it up.
- The **median** is 50. Half the people earn less, half earn more.

Which one describes a typical employee? The median. Reporting the mean here would be technically correct and quite misleading.

## When to use which

- **Mean** works well for fairly symmetric data without extreme values.
- **Median** is better for skewed data such as income, house prices or response times.
- **Mode** suits categories, like the most popular shirt size or the most common ticket type.

## A good habit

Look at the mean and the median together. If they're far apart, your data is skewed or has outliers, and that's worth investigating.

## Try it today

Calculate the mean and median of a column in your own data. If they differ a lot, find out which rows are causing it.
