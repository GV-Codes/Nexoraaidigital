Ice cream sales and sunburns rise and fall together. Does ice cream cause sunburn? Of course not. Hot, sunny weather drives both. That hidden third factor is called a confounder, and it's behind many misleading headlines.

## Three common traps

- **A hidden cause.** Something else drives both, like the weather in the example above.
- **Reverse direction.** Do people who exercise feel happier, or do happier people exercise more? The arrow may point the other way.
- **Coincidence.** With enough data, odd patterns appear by chance.

## How to get closer to causation

- **Run an experiment.** Randomly assign people to a change or a control group, such as an A/B test. It's the gold standard.
- **Control for other factors.** Compare like with like when you can't randomize.
- **Check the timing.** The cause has to come before the effect.
- **Look for a plausible mechanism.** Can you explain how A would lead to B?

## Choose your words

Unless you've done the work, say "is associated with" or "is linked to" rather than "causes." It's accurate, and it protects your credibility.

## Try it today

Think of a recent claim like "customers who use feature X stay longer." List two other explanations that could produce the same pattern.
