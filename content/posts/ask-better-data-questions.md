"How are sales doing?" sounds like a question, but it's really a topic. An analyst who dives straight into the numbers will produce something, though probably not what anyone needed.

## Start with the decision

Ask first: what will someone do differently based on the answer? If nobody will act on it, it may not be worth analyzing.

## Make the question specific

A good analytical question has:

- **A clear metric:** revenue, orders, churn rate
- **A defined group:** which customers, regions or products
- **A time window:** last quarter, since the launch
- **A comparison:** versus last year, versus target, versus another segment

"How are sales doing?" becomes "Did revenue from new customers in the Northeast grow this quarter compared with last quarter, and did we hit the target?"

## Questions to ask the requester

1. What decision does this feed into?
2. Who will use the results?
3. What would you do if the answer were high? What if it were low?
4. How do you define the key terms, such as "active customer"?
5. When do you need it, and how precise does it have to be?

## Why this matters

Definitions are where analyses quietly disagree. Two people can both report "customers" and mean different things.

## Try it today

Take the next data request you receive and reply with three clarifying questions before you open a spreadsheet.
