Gap analysis sounds technical, but it's just a clear way of answering three questions: Where are we now? Where do we want to be? What's in the way?

## The steps

1. **Define the target.** Be specific about what success looks like, such as "support tickets answered within four hours, 95% of the time."
2. **Describe the current state** using facts and data, not impressions. Maybe it's currently 70%.
3. **List the gaps.** What's missing between the two? People, tools, skills, processes, data, policy?
4. **Find causes.** Why does each gap exist?
5. **Plan how to close them,** with owners and timelines.

## A simple layout

| Area | Current state | Target state | Gap | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Response time | 70% within 4 hours | 95% within 4 hours | 25 points | Add weekend coverage |
| Knowledge base | Outdated articles | Reviewed quarterly | No owner | Assign an owner |

## Where it's useful

Choosing software, planning a process change, preparing for an audit or certification, and building a skills plan for a team.

## Common mistakes

- A vague target, so you can't measure the gap
- Describing the current state from memory instead of evidence
- Writing a long list of gaps without ranking them

## Try it today

Pick one goal and fill out a table like the one above with two or three rows. Rank the gaps by impact.
