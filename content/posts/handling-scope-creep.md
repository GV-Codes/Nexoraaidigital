Scope creep is when a project quietly grows: one small request, then another, then another. No single change seems big, but together they blow up the schedule and the budget.

## Why it happens

Usually not bad intent. People remember things they forgot, see new possibilities, or assume a change is trivial. Often the original scope was never clear to begin with.

## Prevention

- **Write the scope down,** including what's out of scope.
- **Get sign-off** from the people who matter.
- **Use clear acceptance criteria,** so "done" isn't a matter of opinion.
- **Agree on a change process** at the start.

## When a request arrives

1. **Don't say yes or no on the spot.** Say, "Thanks, let me understand the impact."
2. **Assess it.** What does it cost in time, money and risk? What does it affect?
3. **Present the trade-offs.** "We can add this, and it moves the launch by two weeks. Or we can swap it for item X. Which would you prefer?"
4. **Let the decision-maker decide,** and record the outcome.

## Keep it positive

Saying "not now" or "here's the cost" is very different from saying "no." You're giving people the information to choose, not blocking them.

## Park good ideas

Keep a list for a future release. People are far more relaxed when their idea is captured rather than dismissed.

## Try it today

Create a simple change request template with four fields: what's requested, why, impact, and decision. Use it for the next request that comes in.
