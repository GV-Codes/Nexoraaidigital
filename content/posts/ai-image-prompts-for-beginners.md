Image generators respond best to prompts that read like a clear brief to a photographer or illustrator. "A dog" gets you an average dog. A little detail gets you a picture.

## A simple formula

Describe these in roughly this order:

1. **Subject:** what's in the picture
2. **Setting:** where it is
3. **Style:** photo, watercolor, flat illustration, 3D render
4. **Composition:** close-up, wide shot, from above
5. **Lighting:** soft morning light, neon, overcast
6. **Mood:** cozy, dramatic, calm

For example: *A golden retriever puppy sleeping on a windowsill during rain, watercolor illustration, soft grey light, cozy mood, close-up.*

## Iterate gently

Change one thing at a time. If you rewrite the whole prompt each round, you'll never know what made the difference.

## Know the limits

- Text inside images is still unreliable. Add words yourself in a design tool.
- Hands, small details and crowds can go wrong. Zoom in before you publish.

## Check before you use it

- Read the tool's license terms for commercial use.
- Don't ask for the style of living artists or for copyrighted characters.
- Say it's AI-generated where that matters to your audience or platform.

## Try it today

Write one prompt using all six parts of the formula, generate it, then change only the lighting and compare.
