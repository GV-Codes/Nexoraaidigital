A password alone is a single lock. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds a second one, usually something you have, like your phone, on top of something you know, like the password. Even if a scammer steals the password, they're stuck.

## The common types, from weakest to strongest

- **Text message codes.** Better than nothing, but they can be intercepted or redirected through SIM-swapping.
- **Authenticator app codes.** A six-digit code that refreshes every 30 seconds. A solid upgrade.
- **Passkeys and hardware security keys.** The strongest option. They're resistant to phishing because they won't work on a fake site.

## Where to turn it on first

1. Your main email account, since it resets everything else
2. Banking and payment apps
3. Your password manager
4. Social media and work accounts

## Habits that matter

- **Save your backup codes** somewhere safe, so a lost phone doesn't lock you out.
- **Never approve a prompt you didn't start.** Attackers sometimes spam login requests hoping you'll tap "Approve" to make it stop. If it happens, deny it and change your password.
- **Never read a code to anyone** who calls or texts you, even if they say they're from your bank.

## Try it today

Switch on MFA for your email account, using an authenticator app or a passkey if it's offered. Print or store the backup codes.
