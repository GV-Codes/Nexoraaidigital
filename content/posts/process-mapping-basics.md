You can't improve a process you can't see. Mapping it on paper often exposes problems in minutes, like steps nobody remembers adding or a handoff where requests go to die.

## The basic symbols

- **Oval:** start or end
- **Rectangle:** a task or action
- **Diamond:** a decision with yes/no branches
- **Arrow:** the flow from one step to the next

## How to draw one

1. **Choose one process** and set clear start and end points, for example "customer places an order" to "order is delivered."
2. **List the steps** in order, using a short verb phrase for each, such as "check stock."
3. **Add decisions** where the path splits.
4. **Note who does each step.** Swim lanes, which are rows for each person or team, make handoffs obvious.
5. **Walk it with the people who do the work** and fix what you got wrong.

## Map today's process first

Start with how things really work now, not how the manual says they should. The gap between the two is where the useful findings are.

## What to look for

- Long waits between steps
- Repeated handoffs
- Rework loops
- Steps that exist for reasons nobody can explain
- Manual work that could be automated

## Try it today

Map one process you do every week on a single page, then circle the step that takes the longest or causes the most frustration.
