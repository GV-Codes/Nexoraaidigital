Your router is the front door to your home network, and most people never touch its settings. Ten minutes can improve things a lot.

## The fixes

1. **Change the admin password.** The default login printed on the device is widely known. Pick a long, unique one.
2. **Use WPA3 or WPA2 for Wi-Fi,** with a long passphrase. Avoid old WEP or open networks.
3. **Update the firmware.** Router makers fix security flaws like any other software. Turn on auto-updates if offered.
4. **Turn off WPS.** The push-button setup feature has known weaknesses.
5. **Set up a guest network** for visitors and smart devices. If a cheap gadget is compromised, it can't reach your laptop.
6. **Disable remote administration** unless you truly need it.
7. **Check what's connected.** Your router's device list shows everything on the network. Remove anything you don't recognize.

## A note on the network name

Avoid putting your name or address in the Wi-Fi name. It tells strangers more than you want.

## If your router is very old

If it no longer gets updates from the manufacturer, replacing it is the best security upgrade you can make.

## Try it today

Log in to your router, change the admin password, and look at the list of connected devices.
