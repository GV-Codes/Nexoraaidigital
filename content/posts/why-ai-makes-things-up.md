Sooner or later, every AI user gets burned. The chatbot gives you a perfect-sounding statistic, a neat quote or a source with a real-looking title, and none of it exists. People call this a hallucination.

## Why it happens

The model is built to produce text that sounds right, not text that has been checked. When it doesn't have a solid answer, it fills the gap with something plausible. It's most likely to do this with obscure facts, recent events, exact numbers, quotes and citations.

## Red flags

- A very specific statistic with no source
- A quote attributed to a named person
- A link, case name or paper title you can't find when you search for it
- An answer that is suspiciously convenient for your argument

## How to catch it

1. **Ask for sources, then open them.** Check that each one exists and actually says what the AI claims.
2. **Give it the material.** Paste in the document and say "answer only from the text below, and tell me if it isn't covered."
3. **Give it permission to say "I don't know."** It makes a real difference.
4. **Ask twice, differently.** If the two answers disagree, neither one is safe.
5. **Match your checking to the stakes.** A brainstorm needs a glance. Anything legal, medical, financial or public needs a real source.

Lawyers have been sanctioned for filing court papers that cited cases an AI invented. Don't be the cautionary tale.

## Try it today

Ask a chatbot for three sources on a niche topic you know well. Look up each one and count how many are real.
