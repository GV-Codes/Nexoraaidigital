Teams love measuring things. The trouble starts when everything gets measured and nobody knows which numbers actually matter.

## The difference

A **metric** is any measurement: page views, calls answered, tickets opened. A **KPI** (key performance indicator) is a metric tied directly to a goal. If your goal is keeping customers, monthly retention is a KPI. Page views probably aren't.

## Watch out for vanity metrics

Vanity metrics look impressive but don't guide decisions, like total sign-ups or social followers with no sense of what they do for the business.

## What makes a good KPI

- **Linked to a goal** you genuinely care about
- **Clearly defined,** so everyone calculates it the same way
- **Actionable,** meaning the team can influence it
- **Owned** by a specific person or team
- **Given a target and a time frame**

## Leading and lagging

Lagging indicators, like revenue, tell you what already happened. Leading indicators, like demo requests or trial activations, hint at what's coming. A healthy set includes both.

## Keep the list short

Three to five KPIs per team is plenty. More than that and focus disappears.

## Try it today

Write down your team's top goal. List the metrics you track and mark which ones directly show progress toward it. Those are your candidate KPIs.
