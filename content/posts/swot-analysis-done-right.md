SWOT is one of the best-known business tools, and one of the most poorly used. Too many are filled with vague statements like "great team" and then filed away. Done properly, it sharpens decisions.

## The four boxes

- **Strengths:** internal advantages
- **Weaknesses:** internal limitations
- **Opportunities:** outside conditions you could use
- **Threats:** outside conditions that could hurt you

The key distinction is that strengths and weaknesses are about *you*, while opportunities and threats come from *outside*.

## Make it useful

- **Be specific.** "Strong brand" says little. "Customers rank us first for reliability in our last two surveys" says a lot.
- **Use evidence.** Back items with data, customer feedback or results.
- **Include different voices.** Sales, support and finance will see different things.
- **Be honest about weaknesses.** Nobody is fooled by a polished list.

## Turn it into action

The real value comes from combining boxes:

- How can strengths help us seize opportunities?
- How can we use strengths to reduce threats?
- Which weaknesses stop us from using an opportunity?
- What's the plan for the most serious threat?

## Don't stop at the list

Pick the top two or three items, assign an owner and decide what happens next.

## Try it today

Run a SWOT on your own team or project, then write down three actions that come from combining boxes.
