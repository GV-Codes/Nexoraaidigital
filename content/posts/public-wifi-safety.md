Public Wi-Fi has a scary reputation, some of it outdated. Most websites now use encrypted HTTPS connections, which blocks a lot of the old snooping tricks. But some risks are still real.

## What can still go wrong

- **Fake hotspots.** Someone sets up a network called "Airport_Free_WiFi" and watches whatever passes through.
- **Sites without HTTPS**, where your activity isn't protected.
- **Login pages that ask for too much.** A free Wi-Fi sign-in has no reason to want your bank details.
- **Nearby devices** on a network with no protection between users.

## Sensible habits

1. **Confirm the network name** with staff before you connect.
2. **Use your phone's hotspot** for anything sensitive. It's usually the safest option.
3. **Look for the padlock** and https in the address bar.
4. **Turn off auto-join** for open networks, so your device doesn't connect on its own.
5. **Keep your device updated** and use multi-factor authentication on important accounts.
6. **Log out** when you finish.

## What about a VPN?

A reputable VPN adds a layer by encrypting your traffic between your device and the VPN provider. It helps, but it isn't magic, and it won't save you from a phishing page you type your password into.

## Try it today

Go to your phone's Wi-Fi settings, delete the old public networks it remembers, and switch off automatic joining.
