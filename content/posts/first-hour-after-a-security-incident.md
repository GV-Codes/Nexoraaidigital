You clicked a link, opened a strange file, or noticed activity you didn't make. Your heart sinks. What you do next matters more than how you got there.

## In the first hour

1. **Stay calm and stop what you're doing.** Don't keep clicking around.
2. **Disconnect the device from the network.** Turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the cable. You don't need to power it off, which can erase useful evidence.
3. **Report it immediately.** At work, tell your IT or security team. Fast reporting limits the damage and nobody will think less of you for it.
4. **Preserve evidence.** Don't delete the email or file. Take screenshots and jot down times and what you saw.
5. **Change passwords from a clean device,** starting with the affected accounts and your email. Turn on multi-factor authentication.
6. **Watch your accounts.** Check for unfamiliar logins, forwarding rules in your email and payment activity.

## What not to do

- Don't try to fix it quietly yourself.
- Don't reply to or confront the attacker.
- Don't wipe the device before the security team says it's fine.
- Don't assume it's too small to report.

## Prepare before it happens

Every organization should have a simple incident plan with names and phone numbers, and everyone should know where to find it.

## Try it today

Find out who you would call at your workplace for a security problem, and save the number in your phone.
