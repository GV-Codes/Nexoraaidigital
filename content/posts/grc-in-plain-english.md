GRC sounds like jargon, but the ideas are down to earth. It stands for governance, risk and compliance, and it's about running an organization sensibly and safely.

## The three parts

**Governance** is who decides what and under which rules. It covers policies, roles and accountability. Who approves new software? Who owns customer data?

**Risk** is the practice of asking what could go wrong, how likely it is, how bad it would be, and what to do about it.

**Compliance** is proving that you follow the rules you're required to follow, whether laws like GDPR or HIPAA, or standards such as ISO 27001, SOC 2 or PCI DSS.

## A small example

Take a lost laptop.

- *Governance:* a policy says company laptops must be encrypted.
- *Risk:* a lost, unencrypted laptop could expose customer data. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high.
- *Compliance:* an auditor asks for evidence that every laptop is encrypted, and you show it.

## The risk register

Most teams track risks in a simple table with the risk, likelihood, impact, owner and planned action. It isn't glamorous, but it's the heart of the work.

## Why the three belong together

Rules without risk thinking become box-ticking. Risk thinking without rules becomes opinion. Compliance without governance is a report nobody acts on.

## Try it today

Write down three things that could go wrong in your team's work. Rate each one's likelihood and impact from 1 to 5, and see which one deserves attention first.
