Scammers aren't usually technical geniuses. They're students of human behavior. Almost every scam leans on the same few psychological pressures, and once you spot them, they're much easier to resist.

## The six levers

**1. Urgency.** "Act in the next hour." Time pressure stops you from thinking. *Counter: any real emergency can survive a short pause.*

**2. Authority.** A message from your boss, the tax office or the police. *Counter: confirm through a number or channel you already trust.*

**3. Fear.** Your account is locked, you owe money, there's a warrant. *Counter: notice the feeling, then verify before you react.*

**4. Reward.** A prize, a refund, an easy job offer. *Counter: if you didn't enter, you didn't win.*

**5. Familiarity.** A friendly stranger, or a message that appears to come from a friend or colleague. *Counter: accounts get hijacked. Check with the person another way.*

**6. Helpfulness.** A small favor first, then a bigger one. *Counter: you can say no to anyone, however polite they are.*

## The common thread

Every lever tries to get you to skip verification. The best defense is a habit: stop, then check through a separate route.

## It happens to smart people

Being tricked says nothing about your intelligence. Scams are designed by professionals and tested on thousands of people.

## Try it today

Look at the last suspicious message you received and find which levers it used.
