It's tempting to paste whatever you're working on into a chatbot and say "help." Before you do, it's worth knowing what to hold back.

## Keep these out

- **Passwords, API keys and access tokens.** No exceptions.
- **Personal data about other people**, such as customer details, health records, or account and ID numbers.
- **Confidential work material**, like unreleased financials, contracts, or source code you're not cleared to share.
- **Private messages** from friends, family or colleagues.
- **Anything covered by an NDA or regulation** such as HIPAA or GDPR.

## Why it matters

Depending on the tool and your settings, what you type may be stored, reviewed by people, or used to improve future models. Even when it isn't, you've still handed information to a third party, and you can't take it back.

## How to get the help anyway

- Swap real details for placeholders: "Client A," "[amount]," "[city]."
- Describe the situation instead of pasting the document.
- Use the AI tool your employer has approved, if there is one. Those usually come with contractual privacy protections.
- Look in your tool's settings for a data or privacy section and turn off training on your conversations if that option exists.

## Try it today

Open the privacy or data controls in the AI tool you use most. Find out whether your chats are used for training, and change the setting if you don't like the answer.
